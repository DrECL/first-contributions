## 2. The Reader

*(Distilled from audience-profiles.md — canonical, 2026-07-19.)*

**Primary:** Macro practitioners and educators — policy advocates, organizers, nonprofit administrators, researchers, social work educators. MSW/DSW-level, geographically dispersed, often the only macro person in the room.
**Secondary:** Macro-curious BSW/MSW students who don't yet know macro is a career path.
**Tertiary:** Adjacent change agents — nonprofit leaders, organizers, public health, civic leaders — doing macro work without the title.

**Their reality:** professional isolation (<20% of MSW students go macro), burnout (79% report symptoms), funding instability, DEI rollbacks, 70% of time lost to paperwork. **They want:** immediately usable tools and frameworks, connection with like-minded practitioners, research in plain language, hope grounded in real success stories, and professional development that fits a crushed schedule.

**Resonates:** personal practitioner stories · "how I did it" case studies · research translated with practice implications · hope-based framing · community wins · tools and templates.
**Falls flat:** purely academic writing · deficit-only content · generic social work content · anything that feels like selling · overly long pieces without clear structure or takeaways.

**Priority topics (2025–2026):** workforce crisis & burnout · DEI rollbacks & federal policy · funding disruptions & nonprofit sustainability · macro identity & visibility ("Macro is Now") · technology, AI & digital transformation.

## 3. Voice

*(Canonical: brand-voice.md, 2026-07-19 — supersedes the older Brand Voice Guide.)*

**Sound like:** a trusted colleague who happens to teach doctoral students; bridges theory and practice; treats every reader as a capable change agent; leads by inviting.
**Never sound like:** detached academic writing · doom-and-gloom advocacy · corporate jargon or motivational fluff · AI filler · gatekeeping.

**Hope-based language transformations (always):** fight against→build toward · at-risk youth→young leaders · vulnerable populations→communities ready for change · underserved→untapped potential · broken system→system ready for transformation · failed policies→opportunities for policy innovation · struggling with poverty→building economic opportunity · systemic barriers→points for systemic change.

**Structure habits:** open with possibility · lead with solutions, then name the challenge · concrete examples, never fabricated · short paragraphs (3–4 sentences) · bold sparingly · Key Takeaways (3) on long-form · end with an invitation, not a lecture · first-person expertise where real ("In my teaching, I've found…").

**Watch-outs (his own words, from the guides):** one generic tip at the end of a deep piece minimizes the real work and reads as AI. This framing is NOT toxic positivity — never minimize real struggle.

## 4. Hope-Based Communication — the five checks

*(From the Hope Based Comms Quick Guide — apply to every issue before it ships.)*

1. **One part problem, two parts solution.** Sell the outcome first, then the process. Make the solution visualizable.
2. **Opportunity, not threat.** Momentum toward change; make inaction the injustice, not the abuse inevitable. Active verbs; name who can fix it.
3. **Say what we stand for,** not what we're against. Frame on our values (fairness, dignity, solidarity) — never debate on the opponent's frame.
4. **Heroes, not victims.** Give readers people to stand WITH, not people to save. Three-dimensional humans with hopes and courage.
5. **Choose the emotion.** Anger + hope + belief that things can get better. Light a candle; don't curse the darkness.

## 5. Issue Structure (locked 2026-07-31)

*(The lean structure from the July settled block — chosen over the six-section format because the audience punishes long unclear pieces and both platforms reward one strong piece + curated links.)*

1. **Subject line + preview text**
2. **Founder's note** — ~150 words, personal, warm
3. **Main thought piece** — 600–900 words, ONE idea done well
4. **Reading / Watching / Waiting** — 3–5 curated links, annotated
5. **Advocacy + CTA** — ~100 words, exactly ONE action
6. **Subscribe / share ask + footer**
7. **LinkedIn version** — ~300 words, hook-first, funnels to the full issue

**Content lens:** the retired six-section framework (The Briefing · Through the Lens · Field Signal · AI and Social Work · Resource Stack · Macro Moment) survives as the rotation menu — each month's main piece takes one lens; Resource Stack lives in Reading/Watching/Waiting; Macro Moment energy goes into the Founder's note or CTA.

## 6. The Monthly Cycle (Dr. Erick + the Coordinator agent)

Fully human-in-the-loop. Dr. Erick authors direction and voice; the agent keeps rhythm and does labor. Agent works Mondays; Dr. Erick's response target is Friday, weekend as built-in catch-up.

| Week | Agent (Monday) | Dr. Erick (Fri–weekend) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reads **Ideas folder first**, then digests + this packet → Planning page (story options, idea triage, ten-minute decision) | Gut check: pick direction, add seed lines, mark GO |
| 2 | First draft from HIS direction only | Red pen |
| 3 | Edits folded in + hope-based pass → near-final | YES on approval line |
| 4 | Publish-ready Substack + LinkedIn text locked | Tweaks only |
| Send-eve Monday | Verify + reminder push | — |
| **First Tuesday** | 6am reminder push | **Publishes. Marks SENT** |
| Next Monday | Files FINAL to Sent Issues, logs to Notion, closes | — |

**Drafting conversations follow the 8-step pipeline** (from newsletter-content-pipeline): topic in his words → editorial direction → storyline approval → his refinements (they are the heart of the piece) → outline review → deeper/spiritual dimension → final outline approval → draft. The agent compresses these into the Planning page and draft rounds but honors the principle: never flatten his idea, never draft past an unanswered gate.

**Idea capture between cycles** (from the Workflow Guide, still his habit): anything good from RSS/Gmail/reading goes into the **Macro Memo Ideas** folder or the Idea Jots doc — 30 seconds, minimum viable note = topic + link.
