# Skill: Newsletter Composer (Macro Memo)

## Purpose
Draft the Macro Memo newsletter — curated insights, original commentary, and community connection for macro-level change agents.

## When to Use
- Monthly newsletter drafting cycle
- When asked to compose or draft a newsletter issue
- When Content Planner has newsletter entries to produce

## Inputs Required
- Recent blog posts, social content, or community updates to feature
- Brand-context files: `C:\Users\macro\.claude\brand\brand-voice.md`, `C:\Users\macro\.claude\brand\audience-profiles.md`, `C:\Users\macro\.claude\brand\products-services.md`
- Any curated external resources or articles to include

## Process

### Step 1: Load Context
1. Read `C:\Users\macro\.claude\brand\brand-voice.md` — newsletter tone: warm, collegial, like writing to a professional friend
2. Read `C:\Users\macro\.claude\brand\audience-profiles.md` — understand what the audience cares about right now
3. Review recent Macro Minded content for items to feature

### Step 2: Plan the Issue
Each Macro Monthly issue follows this structure:

1. **Subject Line** — Generate 3 options. Should be curiosity-driven, specific, and under 50 characters. Avoid clickbait.
2. **Opening Note** (75-150 words) — Personal, warm greeting from Dr. Lear. Sets the theme for this issue. Hope-based framing. Feels like a colleague checking in.
3. **Featured Insight** (150-250 words) — The main content piece. Could be:
   - Original micro-essay on a current topic
   - Key takeaway from a recent blog post with link
   - Research translation (new finding → practice implication)
   - Commentary on a current policy development
4. **Quick Reads** (3-4 items, 30-50 words each) — Curated links with brief commentary:
   - What it is
   - Why it matters for macro practitioners
   - One-sentence takeaway
5. **Community Spotlight** (50-100 words) — Optional. Highlight a practitioner story, student achievement, community win, or upcoming event.
6. **One Actionable Resource** — A tool, template, framework, or guide the reader can use this week.
7. **Closing + CTA** (50-75 words) — Warm sign-off, discussion question or reflection prompt, and reminder of how to connect (community, social, reply to email).

### Step 3: Write the Draft
- Keep total newsletter under 800 words (readers scan, not read)
- Every section should be skimmable — bold key phrases, short paragraphs
- Links should feel curated, not dumped — each one gets a "why you should read this" sentence
- Apply hope-based framing throughout
- Tone: like a trusted colleague sending you the three things worth reading this month

### Step 4: Subject Line Optimization
Generate 3 subject line options using different approaches:
- **Curiosity:** Poses a question or teases an insight
- **Value-first:** States the benefit directly
- **Timely:** References current events or seasonal relevance

All under 50 characters. No ALL CAPS. No excessive punctuation.

### Step 5: Quality Check
- [ ] Subject lines are compelling and under 50 characters
- [ ] Opening note feels personal and warm
- [ ] Featured insight delivers genuine value (not just a summary)
- [ ] Quick reads are truly curated (not filler)
- [ ] Actionable resource is something the reader can use this week
- [ ] Hope-based framing throughout
- [ ] Total length under 800 words
- [ ] Clear CTA at the end
- [ ] Would a busy macro practitioner feel this was worth opening?
