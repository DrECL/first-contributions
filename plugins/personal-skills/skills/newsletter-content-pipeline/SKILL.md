# AI-Assisted Newsletter Content Pipeline

## Overview
This workflow produces a `{content_type}` issue for `{publication_name}` using the `{format_template}` format. It is **human-in-the-loop by design**: the writer drives every stage — topic, tone, narrative arc, outline shape, thematic depth, and final voice — before the agent proceeds to the next step. **Never skip an approval gate. Never proceed autonomously when a topic or direction is missing — stop and ask.**

## Parameters
- **publication_name** (required): The name of the newsletter or publication
- **content_type** (required, default: "newsletter"): The type of content being produced
- **format_template** (optional, default: "Collaborative"): The production format/collaboration style
- **topic** (required): The topic, theme, or idea for this issue in the writer's own words
- **editorial_notes** (optional): Editorial guidance, audience notes, or tone direction
- **framing_notes** (optional): Framing input or thematic lens for the narrative
- **outline_additions** (optional): Additions or refinements requested during outline review
- **spiritual_dimension** (optional): Spiritual, philosophical, or deeper thematic layer to weave through the piece

## Steps

### Step 1/8: Propose Topic
Take the writer's `{topic}` in their own words — often rich, descriptive, and multi-layered. Reflect it back fully and warmly before confirming. Do not reduce or flatten the idea. Ask if anything needs to be added, shifted, or emphasized before moving to research.

---

### Step 2/8: Confirm Topic & Editorial Direction
The writer confirms the topic and provides editorial direction (`{editorial_notes}`). Apply it, then move into research across all relevant theme pillars and return with a curated source list for the writer to review before proceeding.

*Example direction from a past session: "yes, without the why section — my audience knows that" → peer-level, knowing tone; skip explanatory "why" framing.*

---

### Step 3/8: Approve Storyline & Framing
Present a full narrative arc — the emotional and intellectual journey from opening to close — for the writer to review. The writer approves and adds a framing lens (`{framing_notes}`). After approval, present the refined storyline essence back before proceeding to outline.

*Example: a biopsychosocial framework was introduced here as the connective tissue between all pillars.*

---

### Step 4/8: Refine Storyline
The writer may correct or sharpen the narrative essence before the outline begins. Listen carefully for these refinements — they are the heart of the writer's voice. Reflect the revised essence back for confirmation before proceeding.

*Example: the writer distinguished that "spring is not balance — spring is energy and regrowth; the human task is to meet that energy wisely." That correction became the core tension of the piece.*

---

### Step 5/8: Review Outline — Round 1
Present the full structural outline. The writer requests additions, changes, or new threads to research and weave in (`{outline_additions}`). Research any requested additions, then present the revised outline before proceeding.

*Example additions: (1) indigenous cultural wisdom about seasonal connection; (2) individual variation in biological rhythms — honoring that "normal" differs person to person, and that listening inward is the practice.*

---

### Step 6/8: Add Deeper Dimension
The writer adds a deeper thematic, spiritual, or philosophical dimension to weave throughout (`{spiritual_dimension}`). This is not an add-on; it is the ground beneath everything. Reflect the full framework back and confirm before revising the outline.

*Example: the spiritual dimension of the biopsychosocial framework — connection to something greater than the self, the antidote to the "man-made consumption machine."*

---

### Step 7/8: Approve Final Outline
Present the fully revised outline — with all additions, refinements, and the deeper dimension woven throughout — for final approval before drafting begins. **Do not begin drafting until the writer explicitly approves this structure.**

---

### Step 8/8: Draft & Review
Write the full draft true to the approved outline, the writer's voice, and all confirmed framing. Tone: warm, unhurried, peer-level — science woven in as texture, wisdom held with respect, deeper dimension felt rather than argued, ending on an open door rather than a conclusion. After delivering the draft, hand it back to the writer to read, mark up, and rewrite what doesn't sound like them. The agent remains available to fill gaps, suggest alternatives, and check consistency — but this stage belongs to the writer.
